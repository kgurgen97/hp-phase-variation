# Cover letter draft — Microbial Genomics

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “A reproducible short-read framework for profiling repeat-length variation at candidate phase-variable loci in *Helicobacter pylori*,” for publication in *Microbial Genomics* as a Technical Resource.

Repeat-mediated variation is biologically important in *H. pylori*, but short-read analysis is complicated by repetitive sequence, uncertain locus boundaries, orthology ambiguity, sequencing noise, and strong bacterial population structure. We developed a conservative workflow that explicitly separates repeat-locus discovery, evidence for phase variation, technical callability, and eligibility for downstream association analysis.

The manuscript presents a multi-reference catalogue, a frozen repeat-length caller, boundary/orthology and callability audits, empirical synthetic benchmarking, and independent high-accuracy long-read validation. Among callable comparisons, the caller showed high exact repeat-length concordance, including for observed non-reference alleles. Equally important, the framework retains uncallable and ambiguous loci rather than treating all catalogue loci as measurable.

We also applied the frozen framework to public gastric-disease cohorts as a stress test. Under pre-specified rules, the available cohorts did not support the intended strict disease-association endpoint. We report this limitation directly and do not interpret diagnostic analyses as evidence for a gastric-cancer association. We believe this combination of technical validation, explicit failure modes, and reproducible negative identifiability assessment will be useful to researchers applying microbial genomics to repeat-rich loci and heterogeneous public datasets.

The work fits the journal’s scope in genomic methodologies, microbial population genomics, and *Helicobacter* research. The publication package includes frozen analysis code, manifests, machine-audited manuscript numbers, and reproducible figure generation. All underlying sequencing datasets are public and identified by accession.
