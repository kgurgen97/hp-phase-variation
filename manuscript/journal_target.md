# Journal target assessment

## Primary target: Microbial Genomics

The current manuscript is best framed as a **Technical Resource / genomic methodology paper** rather than a gastric-cancer association paper.

Fit:
- microbial genomic methodology and benchmarking;
- *Helicobacter pylori* comparative/population-genomic context;
- explicit treatment of population structure and phylogeography;
- reproducible code/data package;
- technical validation plus a restrained real-world pilot application;
- journal scope explicitly includes genomic methodologies and population structure/phylogeography, and the journal hosts a Campylobacter/Helicobacter genomics collection.

Submission-facing requirements to satisfy before upload:
- Word manuscript with line numbers;
- Data Summary;
- Impact Statement;
- Funding statement;
- Conflict-of-interest statement;
- separate figure files;
- supplementary material;
- public or review-accessible supporting data/code, with raw sequencing accessions public;
- final author/affiliation metadata;
- persistent release/DOI for the publication repository if feasible.

## Backup target: mSystems

Potential article types are Resource Report or Methods and Protocols. The journal accepts format-neutral initial submissions and strongly emphasizes reproducible data/code.

Main disadvantage for this frozen study: its Methods and Protocols criteria explicitly expect performance comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods. Adding a new caller-comparison benchmark now would expand the frozen analytical scope, so mSystems is a less natural first target unless editors view the resource/validation package as sufficient without additional analysis.

## Framing decision

Do not market the study as evidence of a gastric-cancer association. Lead with:
1. repeat-locus catalogue and locus-definition audit;
2. conservative short-read allele caller;
3. synthetic and HiFi validation;
4. technical-universe construction and reproducibility;
5. public-cohort application as an identifiability/confounding stress test.

The central gastric-carcinogenesis hypothesis remains unresolved.
